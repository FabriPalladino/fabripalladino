After a long assignment, you get comfortable. You forget what being a freelancer really means.

I did.

Being independent is too often treated as a workaround. A tax trick, a way around an employment contract.

It isn't. Being a freelancer is a bet: which skills to focus on, where your edge in the market is, and how to use it. It is what I love about it, and the hard part.

That bet is what a company gets when it brings one in.

A freelancer moves with agility inside a company's structure, identifies hidden needs and leverages a team's strengths. It is never about what you can build. It is about why you build it, and how. Context is everything.

Coming back to the Brussels market woke me up. Here, much institutional work is commissioned through large framework contracts held by established suppliers and consortia. Those contracts buy days, not judgement. They reward scale and the ability to fill predefined roles, and a freelancer's edge disappears into a standard profile and a daily rate.

This is the distortion: winning the contract is separated from the expertise that delivers the work. Access to the client is decided upstream. The freelancer competes on price downstream.

An edge like that takes investment: time spent learning, choosing a direction, and taking responsibility for those choices.

If you buy through a framework, look for it: ask who the person is, not what the profile says. And we freelancers have to keep earning it.

So this is a reminder, to you and to me, of why freelancers matter.

Now, back to work. I have an edge to hone.
