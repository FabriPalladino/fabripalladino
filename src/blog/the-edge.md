I ended my last post with an edge to hone. Here is where I'm putting the work.

Being a freelancer is a bet: you choose where to invest your time, without knowing whether the market will reward it.

My current bet has two parts.

The first is upstream: deepening my understanding of UX and UI, where decisions about what to build take shape.

What is someone trying to do? What gets in their way? Why would this feature help?

And sometimes: should we build this at all?

Every feature needs a reason to exist, and being able to build it isn't reason enough. I want to get better at recognising when adding something makes a product harder to use.

The second is under the hood: going deeper into the fundamentals of front-end development.

As AI takes on more of the implementation, I want a clearer understanding of what happens underneath. How the browser works. How data moves. What makes an interface responsive, accessible, and reliable.

Enough understanding to question a proposed solution, recognise its trade-offs, and take responsibility for choosing it.

Technical communication connects these two bets.

We need a clear map in our heads of why we are building something and how it should work. Then we need to make that map understandable to someone else: a designer, a developer, a client.

That was already one of the hardest parts of the job. I expect it to become an even larger part of mine.

So this is the edge I'm honing: understanding the problem well enough to question what we build, and understanding the technology well enough to explain how.

Sometimes the answer is no. Sometimes the right choice is to leave some space.
